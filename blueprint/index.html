09WHAT TO WRITE NEXT
Mini Book Writing System · 1 of 2
Your outline is done. Now you just fill the gaps.
Day 1 is finished. The promise, the outline, the key points, the sub-points are all in this blueprint. You have no blank page ahead of you.
What's left is connecting dots you already placed.
THE 7-DAY SCHEDULE
Day 1 — Foundation ✓ Done in your blueprint
Title, subtitle, cover direction, POWER promise, outline, key points, sub-points.
Days 2–6 — Write
1,500 words a day. Two to three hours.
Day 7 — Finish and polish
Fill gaps, smooth transitions, light edit, add the lead magnet.
HOW TO CONNECT THE DOTS
Open the outline. Find today's chapter. Look at key point one and its sub-points.
Type the opening sentence. Then explain the sub-point like you'd explain it out loud to one person who asked.
When you finish a sub-point, move to the next. When you finish the key point, move to the next. Three key points and the chapter is done.
You are never writing from nothing. You're expanding a point that's already written down.
When you stall, switch to voice. Talk the section out loud into voice-to-text, then clean it up. You already know this material. The problem is typing, not thinking.
Don't edit while you write. Day 7 is for editing. Days 2 through 6 are for words on the page.
GUARANTEE THE DRAFT GETS DONE
97% of people who start a book never finish it. These are the five things that put you in the other 3%.
Set a time. Same time every day, written down. A plan you didn't schedule isn't a plan.
Take the early hours. Only 8% of Americans are awake by 5am. Nobody needs anything from you at 5:30.
Thirty minutes still works. It won't be seven days. It'll be a few months. The book still gets written.
Let your subconscious work. Once the outline exists, your brain keeps solving it while you eat, drive, and sleep. That's why Day 1 mattered.
Announce it. Post that you're writing it. Name the finish date. Post updates. Public commitment finishes books that private intentions don't.

THE CONCLUSIONThree moves, in order.
How it ends
01SUM UP
What they now know that they didn't when they started. Restate the argument in its shortest form.
Not a recap of every chapter. The one idea, said once more.
02SEND OFF
Tell them what to do next. Point them at the first action, the next book, the program, the download. A reader who finishes and doesn't know what to do next does nothing.
03ASK FOR THE REVIEW
Directly. Reviews are how the next reader decides whether the book is worth their time and money.
Ask once, plainly, and tell them where.

THE INTRODUCTIONThree moves, in order.
How it opens
01PERSON
Who this book is for, said plainly enough that the right reader recognizes themselves and the wrong one puts it down.
Name them. The founder sitting on a category nobody has heard of. The consultant explaining the same idea in every meeting. Whoever it is, say it so they know.
02PROMISE
What they'll be able to do when they finish. This is your POWER Promise, stated in a paragraph instead of on the cover.
One transformation. Not a list of everything the book covers.
03PREVIEW
What's coming and in what order. A short walk through the shape of the book so the reader knows where they're being taken.
Keep it to a few sentences. This is a map, not a summary.

STEP 5MAKE IT LOOK VALUABLE
Readers judge lead magnets the way they judge covers. A plain Word doc gets ignored.
Build it in Canva, with AI, or even hire a designer. Make it look valuable. Then make a 3D mockup so it feels like a thing instead of a file.
Tools: Canva, MockUp Shots, Placeit, Claude Design, ChatGPT, etc.
STEP 6DELIVER IT
Starting out: put your email address in the book. “Email me and I’ll send it.” Personal, and you get to follow up by hand.
Once it’s working: automate it with Kit, Mailchimp, or Substack.
Either way:
☐Arrives instantly
☐Instructions are obvious
☐You went through it yourself as a reader and fixed anything clunky
