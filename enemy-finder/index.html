Your enemy, named.

    Say the one you starred out loud. If it makes you a little mad, you've found it.

    A book without an enemy is just information.

    Readers don't rally around a topic. They rally against something. The diet everybody's still on. The advice their boss keeps repeating. The thing quietly eating their business while they follow the rules.

    Name that, and your book stops being one more title in a crowded category and starts being the one that finally said it.

    How it works

    1

    Fill in three blanks

    Who you're writing to, what they're stuck on, and your take on fixing it. Rough is fine.

    2

    Answer one question at a time

    Never two. Each answer reworks the board: weak names drop off, sharper ones arrive.

    3

    Star the ones that hit

    A starred name is locked. It never gets dropped, reworded or tidied up. Everything else keeps moving.

    Two lists, two jobs

    The normal way,
    and what gets them.

    The normal way is the conventional wisdom you're fighting. What everyone in your world tells your reader to do, that quietly doesn't work.

    What gets them is the consequence lurking behind it. What happens to your person if they never solve this, that they can't see coming yet.

    You need both. One gives your book something to argue with. The other gives your reader a reason to keep turning pages.
